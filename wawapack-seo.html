Challenge

A new marketplace entered a crowded, price-sensitive category.

Wawapack launched as a one-stop source for packaging across plastic, paper, metal, glass, natural materials, labels, and shipping formats. Competitors were already investing in Google and Facebook advertising.

With many similar products and buyer needs, the main SEO risk was structural: without a clear taxonomy, pages would compete with one another and search engines would struggle to understand the catalog.

My ownership

I made SEO operable by the SEO team—not dependent on every developer request.

Product requirement

Identified the custom site's editing limitation and specified CMS controls so I could make on-page changes quickly and independently.

SEO execution

Owned taxonomy, technical requirements, keyword mapping, metadata, headings, internal links, schema, sitemap, and performance work.

Content operation

Researched keywords by product group, wrote content directly, and managed additional writers as the catalog expanded.

Strategy

Design the search architecture before scaling the catalog.

Architecture

Organize products around type and buyer intent

Created a hub-and-spoke structure so category hubs could guide users and search engines toward specific packaging needs.

Technical SEO

Make every growth layer crawlable

Implemented a clean URL system, XML sitemap, structured data, and Core Web Vitals improvements from the beginning.

Thai demand

Map local language to commercial intent

Conducted Thai keyword research across plastic, paper, eco-packaging, and related high-intent product searches.

On-page system

Standardize relevance across a growing inventory

Optimized titles, metadata, headings, internal links, and schema so new pages inherited a consistent search foundation.

What this demonstrates

For a large catalog, information architecture is not housekeeping—it is the growth strategy.
